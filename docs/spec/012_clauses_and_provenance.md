# 012 — Clauses and provenance

**Status:** draft (2026-10-23). Meaning fixed by D4 and Law 8; the spellings are the plan's (§2.2.5) and await Eric's wording. Implementation: compliant (Phase 2's third slice, landed the same day); every statement has a passing conformance test. The literate `.charter.md` container of D4 is Phase 5.

Every norm carries its English, and the English leads (Law 8). A program names its authoritative document, quotes that document clause by clause, classifies every span of a clause into a register, and says which rule each normative span means. The checks below are run by `charter check` and at the end of `charter test`; each is a *claim* tagged *checked*, about *provenance*, and a failed one fails the file.

**12.1 Document.** `Document [ID] "path" [SHA256 "hex"] [Rank n]` names an authoritative English source relative to the program's file, optionally tagging it with a document identifier `ID` (for multi-document programs), pinning its expected 64-character lowercase SHA-256 content digest (`T39`), and declaring an explicit integer authority rank `n` (`docs/rfc.md` §4.5). `test/spec/spec_012_1_clauses_resolve.eflint`, `spec_012_9_multidoc_sha256.charter`, `spec_012_9b_stale_sha256_drift.charter`

**12.2 Clauses.** `Clause ID [In DOC_ID] [Approved (HASH | "sha256-hex")]` declares a clause; `ID` is atoms and identifiers joined by dots (`DEF.01`, `T.2`). Optional `In DOC_ID` binds the clause to a specific declared `Document DOC_ID`. `Approved` accepts either a 64-character lowercase SHA-256 hex digest string (`"..."`, collision-resistant, `T39`) or a legacy unsigned 64-bit FNV-1a integer. Its items are `Text "…"` (the clause's text; when absent, the spans concatenated) and register spans: `Norm "…" => rule, rule`, `Definition "…" [=> ids]`, `Invariant "…" => invariant`, `Acceptance "…"`, `Commentary "…"`. Spans are in document order. Declaring a clause id twice replaces the first. `test/spec/spec_012_1_clauses_resolve.eflint`, `spec_012_9_multidoc_sha256.charter`

**12.3 `From`.** An act, duty, immunity, invariant, or scenario (D10) may carry `From CLAUSE "span"`: the English it is the meaning of. The clause must be declared and must contain the quoted text. `test/spec/spec_012_1_clauses_resolve.eflint`, `spec_012_3b_invariant_from.eflint`, `spec_012_3c_scenario_from.eflint`

**12.4 Coverage.** A clause's spans, concatenated in order, reproduce its text exactly. `test/spec/spec_012_3_coverage.eflint`

**12.5 Norms are interpreted.** Every `Norm` span names at least one rule, and every id a `Norm` or `Invariant` span names is a declared act, duty, immunity, or invariant; an `Invariant` span names a declared `Invariant`; an `Acceptance`, `Definition`, or `Commentary` span may also name a declared `Scenario` (D10). `test/spec/spec_012_4_norm_needs_rule.eflint`, `spec_012_7_invariant_span.eflint`, `spec_012_3c_scenario_from.eflint`

**12.6 Every rule has its English.** Every declared act, duty, immunity, and invariant is named by some span, in a clause or by its own `From`. A rule with no span is the question and the rule is the answer under review (Law 8). `test/spec/spec_012_5_every_rule_named.eflint`

**12.7 Verbatim.** Each clause's text occurs verbatim in the document. A document that cannot be read is a warning, not a failure, and the check is skipped. `test/spec/spec_012_6_verbatim.eflint`

**12.8 Approval.** `Approved HASH` records the hash of the clause's text when its spans and meanings were reviewed. A clause whose text hashes to its approval is current; one with no approval, or whose text has drifted, is STALE: the report prints the hash to declare once reviewed, and names the rules whose reviewed status the drift invalidates. The English is never edited to fit a rule, and an `Approved` value is never copied to silence the check (`AGENTS.md`). `test/spec/spec_012_2_approved_hash.eflint`

**12.9 Tracing and Markdown anchors.** `charter trace <clause> <file>` prints the clause's span table: register, text, and the rules each span means, with the document tag, approval state, SHA-256 digest, and legacy FNV-1a hash. `charter build` records each clause's nearest preceding Markdown heading (`# ...`), 1-indexed line, and surrounding-context SHA-256 fingerprint in `Artifact.SourceManifest`, keeping verbatim grounding resilient to line shifts elsewhere in the file.

**12.10 Provenance coverage (`complete | partial | illustrative`) and `--require-provenance`.** Every resolved program has a machine-readable provenance classification: `complete` (every normative rule is grounded, every declared `Document` is readable and matches any `SHA256` pin, and every `Clause` check passes without warning), `partial` (some provenance is declared or some rule/clause/document is incomplete, unread, or `STALE`), or `illustrative` (neither `Document` nor `Clause` is declared for the program's rules). Passing `--require-provenance` (or `//! require-provenance`) raises `CHR029` whenever normative rules are declared and provenance coverage is not `complete` (`docs/rfc.md` §4.5, §12.1, T08). `test/spec/spec_012_8_require_provenance.charter`, `spec_012_8b_partial_provenance_rejected.charter`

*Reused from v0.3:* the records `Clause`, `Span`, `Register` and the functions `covered`, `normsInterpreted`, `reverseProvenanceWith`, `stale`, `invalidated` in `src/Charter/Core/Provenance.lean` are the same definitions the v0.3 checker used (Law 13: one definition).
