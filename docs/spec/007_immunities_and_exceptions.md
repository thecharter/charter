# 007 — Immunities and exceptions

**Status:** approved (Eric, 2026-11-06; Charter 1.0.0). Fixed by Law 4, D11, D23, and D25. Implementation: compliant (`src/Charter/Eval/Eval.lean`, `src/Charter/Core/Defeasible.lean`).

Law 4 names four mechanisms with four spellings, and forbids writing one as another:

**7.1 Scope conditions.** A `Conditioned by` or `Holds when` on an act says when it is lawful (005.5). This is eFLINT's mechanism and the only one in Phase 1.

**7.2 Authority exceptions.** `Act x … Except under a₁, a₂`: performing an instance of `x` is lawful when and only when some instance of a named act, sharing the components they have in common (its other components bound to every instance), is enabled and valid. `x`'s own `Holds when` and `Conditioned by` are not consulted (declaring both `Except under` and `Holds when` emits warning `CHR042`, `#13`). The named acts must be acts (CHR022). `test/spec/spec_007_2_except_under.eflint`, `spec_007_2b_except_under_with_holds_when_warns.charter`, `spec_007_4c_against_non_act.eflint`

**7.3 Duty-over-duty precedence and `Excused when` (`D35`, `#57`).** `Duty d … Overridden by d₁, d₂` declares that the named duties take precedence over `d` (conversely, `Duty d₁ … Overrides d[(args)]` declares the precedence on the overriding duty, with optional explicit argument mapping when parties swap roles); `Excused when c` on `Duty` or `Extend Duty` suspends violation while `c` holds without terminating `d`. An instance of `d` is not violated (006.2) while excused or while an overriding duty holds. Precedence is declared, never inferred, and one level deep: the overriding duty's own `Overridden by` is not consulted (the v0.3 field `overriddenBy`, whose reading this is). The named types must be duties (CHR026). `Violated(d(…))` answers accordingly, so a report of `d`'s violation (6.5) is withheld while it is overridden and made when the overriding instance ends. `test/spec/spec_007_3_overridden_by.eflint`, `spec_007_3b_overridden_non_duty.eflint`, `spec_007_3c_duty_overrides_and_excused_when.charter`

**7.4 Immunities.** `Immunity name Holder v [Related to …] Against a₁, a₂ [Holds when e] [Unless e]` declares a type whose instances hold like facts (asserted or derived; an immunity with neither `Holds when` nor any creator warns `CHR041`, `#15`; an immunity whose domain is not enumerable is judged for the instance with its components bound, as 005.5 judges acts). While an instance holds, an act named in `Against` whose `Recipient` is the instance's holder—and whose `Related to` components match the components the two share in common, by name (as 7.2 and 7.3 share them)—is not valid (005.9); it still occurs and is judged lawful or not on its own. `Unless e₁, e₂`, over the immunity's components, lifts the protection while one holds. An act without a `Recipient` is not affected except for `Immunity ... Against Amend` (014.5, D11). The named acts must be acts (CHR022). The immunity is a record, so the protection-over-power edge is data for every projection (Law 5); entrenchment is an immunity against `Amend` (D11, D25). `test/spec/spec_007_4_immunity.eflint`, `spec_007_4b_immunity_unless.eflint`, `spec_007_4d_immunity_related_to.eflint`, `spec_007_4e_uncreated_immunity_warns.charter`

**7.7 Protections (`D35`, `#52`).** `Protection name Holder v [Related to …] For a₁, a₂ [Holds when e] [Unless u]` is the dual of `Immunity` (defaulting to `Holds when True` when omitted): while an un-lifted instance holds for an act's `Recipient` (and no `Immunity` blocks it), performing that act takes valid institutional effect even when its ordinary `Valid when` fails (for example, apparent authority or a good-faith buyer without notice), while `lawful` continues to judge the actor independently (`Law 2`). `test/spec/spec_007_7_protection_dual_of_immunity.charter`

**7.5 Remedies are not exceptions.** A breach's consequences are remedies of a finding (006.6), never a change to what is lawful.

**7.6 Statutory defaults, chained exceptions, and normative conflicts (`D23`, `G3.3`).** Statutory default-and-exception hierarchies over derived facts and functions use `Default`, `Exception … Overrides …`, and `Priority … Over …` (004.12), distinct from act scope conditions (7.1), authority exceptions (7.2), duty precedence (7.3), and immunities (7.4). Multi-level exception-to-exception chains resolve deterministically via transitive dominance (`T22`), priority cycles fail statically with `CHR034`, and unranked overlapping rules with incompatible outcomes produce an explicit normative `Conflict` rather than last-rule-wins (`T21`). `test/spec/spec_007_6_defeasible_exception_dag.charter`, `spec_007_6b_defeasible_unranked_conflict.charter`, `spec_007_6c_defeasible_priority_cycle.charter`
